How it's built

  1. Win %. A win over an FCS team counts as 0.4 of a win here. A loss to an FCS team counts in full. The W-L column shows the real record; * marks teams with an FCS win.
  2. Opponents' win % (OWP). Each opponent's record, with its game against you removed so your result doesn't inflate or deflate it.
  3. Opponents' opponents' win % (OOWP). The average OWP of everyone you played. This catches a 6-1 opponent whose wins came against nobody.
  4. Opponent conference strength. Each conference is rated by its non-conference record vs FBS teams (shrunk toward .500 early). You get the average rating of your opponents' conferences.
  5. Yards per play (opponent-adjusted). For each game vs an FBS team: how many more yards per play you gained than that opponent usually allows, plus how many fewer you allowed than that opponent usually gains. This rewards teams that consistently outplay opponents snap by snap, which predicts future results better than the scoreboard alone. FCS games are left out.
  6. Against the spread. For each game: actual margin plus the team's ESPN line (DraftKings). Beating a 7-point spread by 10 is +3. Capped at ±21 per game so one fluke doesn't dominate.
  7. Margin. Point differential per game, capped at 24 so running up the score earns nothing extra. FCS games are discounted here and in the spread factor.
  8. Quality results. Bonus for wins over teams rated top 10, 11–25 and 26–50; penalty for losses to teams rated 51+ and to FCS teams.

Default weights: win % 27, opponents' win % 19, yards per play 23, opponents' opponents' win % 8, conference strength 8, against the spread 8, margin 4, quality results 3. Tested on the 2024 and 2025 seasons: this mix picked 67.6% of later games correctly vs 64.1% for record alone, and beat the AP poll on games involving ranked teams (75.1% vs 73.2%). FCS opponents are treated as a .300 team in a weak conference. SOS rank combines OWP and OOWP at 2:1.

How the projection works

  1. Team ratings in points. Half comes from performance: opponent-adjusted scoring margin (capped at 28 a game) and opponent-adjusted yards per play, converted to points, weighted 1:3 because yards per play predicted better. The other half comes from the betting market: the team strengths that best explain the ESPN point spreads of games already played. Home field is worth about 3 points.
  2. Win chance for each remaining game. From the rating gap, assuming real margins land within about 15 points of the prediction two times in three. When ESPN has posted a line for the game, the line is used instead.
  3. Simulate the rest of the season. Every remaining regular-season game is played out thousands of times, with each team's rating also allowed to be a bit off in each run. Each finished season is ranked by this poll at its default weights, and teams are ordered by their average final rank.

Tested on 2026 games, fitting only on earlier weeks (weeks 4–9, 308 games): the ratings picked 66% of winners, and the ESPN line picked 72%. That gap narrowed from early October onward as the ratings got more games, which is why posted lines are used for next week's games.

Data: final scores, box-score yards and plays, and ESPN pregame spreads for all __NGAMES__ completed games involving FBS teams. Records are checked against official standings for all __NTEAMS__ teams on every update.
